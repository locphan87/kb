Home Loan

This is a glossary of Australian home loan terms.

LVR (Loan-to-Value Ratio)​

Definition
The loan amount divided by the property value, expressed as a percentage. A higher LVR generally means higher lender risk.

Example
If you borrow $720,000 on a $900,000 home, LVR = 720,000 / 900,000 = 80%.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart (home loans): https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

LMI (Lenders Mortgage Insurance)​

Definition
Insurance that protects the lender (not the borrower) if the borrower defaults. It’s commonly required when the LVR is above a lender threshold (often 80%).

Example
Buying with a 10% deposit may trigger LMI depending on the lender and product.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart (home loans): https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

HEM (Household Expenditure Measure)​

Definition
A benchmark used by many lenders to estimate minimum living expenses when assessing serviceability, based on household characteristics.

Example
A lender may use HEM as a floor even if stated expenses are lower.

References

  • APRA (supervision and lending guidance): https://www.apra.gov.au/

DTI (Debt-to-Income Ratio)​

Definition
Total debt divided by gross annual income. Used by lenders and regulators as a risk indicator.

Example
If total debt is $900,000 and gross income is $150,000, DTI = 6.0.

References

  • APRA (risk and guidance materials): https://www.apra.gov.au/

STP (Straight-Through Processing)​

Definition
Automation where data and documents flow through a lender’s systems with minimal manual touchpoints (e.g., rules-based checks, automated verification, and workflow routing). In home lending, STP aims to reduce turnaround times and rework.

Example
Uploading payslips and bank statements that are automatically read, validated, and routed to approval without manual data entry.

References

  • ASIC (home loan and borrowing guidance): https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans
  • APRA: https://www.apra.gov.au/

OCR (Optical Character Recognition)​

Definition
Technology that converts text in images or scanned PDFs into machine-readable text so it can be searched, extracted, and validated (often used for payslips, ID documents, and statements).

Example
Extracting employer name, YTD income, and pay frequency from a scanned payslip to populate a loan application.

References

  • OAIC (privacy and handling personal information): https://www.oaic.gov.au/

KYC (Know Your Customer)​

Definition
Identity verification and customer due diligence performed by financial institutions to help prevent money laundering and terrorism financing, and to meet regulatory obligations (requirements and procedures vary by product and provider).

Example
Verifying identity using a passport and driver licence, plus verifying source of funds for a large deposit.

References

  • AUSTRAC (AML/CTF and customer due diligence): https://www.austrac.gov.au/

AVM (Automated Valuation Model)​

Definition
A statistical/model-based property valuation estimate generated from data (e.g., recent sales, property attributes, and market trends). Lenders may use AVMs for certain scenarios, often subject to LVR/property-type limits.

Example
A refinance request is assessed using an AVM instead of a full in-person valuation to confirm property value.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart (home loans): https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Credit Bureau​

Definition
An organisation that collects and provides consumer credit information (credit reporting data) to lenders and other authorised entities, subject to privacy and credit reporting rules.

Example
A lender requests a credit report to see enquiries and repayment history information as part of the home loan assessment.

References

  • OAIC (credit reporting): https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/credit-reporting

Serviceability​

Definition
A lender’s assessment of whether you can afford repayments now and in the future, typically using stressed interest rates and verified income/expenses.

Example
You may “pass” at 6.5% even if the actual rate is 5.9%.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart (home loans): https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Serviceability Buffer​

Definition
An additional percentage added to the loan rate used for assessment to test affordability if rates rise.

Example
If the rate is 6.0% and the buffer is 3.0%, the assessment rate may be ~9.0% (subject to lender policy).

References

  • APRA: https://www.apra.gov.au/

Assessment Rate​

Definition
The interest rate a lender uses to test affordability (often the higher of the actual rate + buffer, or a minimum floor rate).

Example
A borrower might repay at 6.1% but be assessed at 9.1%.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Principal and Interest (P&I)​

Definition
Repayments that reduce both the loan balance (principal) and pay interest.

Example
Most owner-occupier home loans are P&I once any interest-only period ends.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Interest-Only (IO)​

Definition
Repayments cover interest only for a set period; the principal doesn’t reduce during that period.

Example
An investor might choose 5 years IO, then revert to P&I.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Fixed Rate​

Definition
An interest rate locked for a set period (e.g., 1–5 years). Break costs may apply if you exit early.

Example
Fixing for 2 years can protect against rate rises during that period.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Variable Rate​

Definition
An interest rate that can change over time, typically moving with the lender’s pricing and market rates.

Example
Your repayment may increase after a lender rate rise.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Split Loan​

Definition
A loan structured with part fixed and part variable to balance certainty and flexibility.

Example
50% fixed for 2 years + 50% variable with offset.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Offset Account​

Definition
A transaction account linked to a home loan where the balance reduces the interest calculated (offsets the loan balance).

Example
$30,000 in offset against a $600,000 loan means interest is charged as if the balance were $570,000.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Redraw Facility​

Definition
Allows you to withdraw extra repayments you’ve made above the minimum (subject to lender rules and limits).

Example
Paying extra over 2 years then redrawing for renovations.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Repayments (Minimum)​

Definition
The scheduled amount required by the lender each period to keep the loan in good standing.

Example
Minimum monthly repayments on a P&I loan typically change if the rate changes.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Extra Repayments​

Definition
Payments above the minimum, which can reduce interest paid and shorten the loan term (subject to product rules).

Example
Adding $100/week can materially reduce total interest over 30 years.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Loan Term​

Definition
The length of time to repay the loan (commonly up to 30 years in Australia).

Example
A 25-year term generally has higher repayments than a 30-year term for the same loan amount.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Amortisation​

Definition
The process of repaying a loan over time through scheduled payments, where the interest component typically decreases and principal component increases over time.

Example
Early repayments on a 30-year P&I loan are mostly interest.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Comparison Rate​

Definition
A rate designed to help compare loans by combining the interest rate with most fees and charges into a single figure (for a standard loan amount/term).

Example
Two loans with the same headline rate can have different comparison rates due to fees.

References

  • ASIC (comparison rate guidance): https://asic.gov.au/

Introductory Rate (Honeymoon Rate)​

Definition
A discounted rate for an initial period that later reverts to a higher ongoing rate.

Example
Discounted for 12 months, then reverts to the standard variable rate.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Discounted Variable Rate​

Definition
A variable rate reduced by a discount off the lender’s reference rate, sometimes for a set time or ongoing.

Example
“Reference rate 8.50% minus 2.40% discount = 6.10% variable.”

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Reference Rate​

Definition
A lender’s benchmark rate from which discounts may be applied; not necessarily the rate borrowers actually pay.

Example
Your contract may state “reference rate less discount.”

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Break Costs​

Definition
Fees that may apply when you repay, refinance, or change a fixed-rate loan during the fixed period.

Example
Refinancing a fixed loan mid-term can trigger break costs depending on market rates and lender method.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Discharge (Mortgage Discharge)​

Definition
The process of removing the lender’s security interest (mortgage) from the property title once the loan is repaid or refinanced.

Example
When refinancing, the old lender discharges the mortgage so the new lender can register theirs.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Discharge Fee​

Definition
A fee charged by some lenders to process closing the loan and discharging the mortgage.

Example
Sometimes called “settlement fee” or “loan closure fee.”

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Annual Package Fee​

Definition
An annual fee that may bundle benefits such as fee waivers, discounts, offset eligibility, or credit card perks.

Example
A package might waive monthly account fees and provide a rate discount.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Establishment Fee​

Definition
An upfront fee charged by some lenders for setting up a new loan.

Example
Often paid at settlement or added to the loan.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Ongoing Fees (Monthly/Annual)​

Definition
Fees charged regularly for maintaining the loan account or package.

Example
Monthly service fees may apply for some basic loans (many lenders advertise “no monthly fee”).

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Application Fee​

Definition
A fee charged to process a loan application (sometimes waived or included within establishment fees).

Example
Some lenders advertise $0 application fee but may have other fees.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Valuation​

Definition
An estimate of property value used by a lender to assess LVR and risk. It may differ from the purchase price.

Example
If valuation comes in low, you may need a bigger deposit or pay LMI.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Mortgage​

Definition
A legal security interest registered on the property title in favour of the lender.

Example
If the borrower defaults, the lender can enforce the security subject to law and process.

References

  • State/Territory land titles authority (varies by state)

Security Property​

Definition
The property used as collateral for the loan.

Example
For an owner-occupied loan, the security is often your home.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Owner-Occupier​

Definition
A borrower who lives in the property as their primary residence, typically eligible for different rates/policies than investors.

Example
An owner-occupier may get a lower rate than an investor loan for the same LVR.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Investor​

Definition
A borrower purchasing or holding property primarily for rental income and/or capital growth rather than living in it.

Example
An investment loan may have different pricing and assessment rules.

References

  • ATO (rental property): https://www.ato.gov.au/

Principal Place of Residence (PPOR)​

Definition
The home you primarily live in; this matters for tax, lending policy, and some state-based concessions.

Example
Some states offer stamp duty concessions for eligible PPOR buyers.

References

  • State/Territory revenue office (stamp duty guidance varies)

First Home Buyer (FHB)​

Definition
Someone buying their first home (eligibility for grants/concessions depends on state rules and conditions).

Example
You may qualify for a first home owner grant depending on state, property type, and value caps.

References

  • State/Territory first home owner grant pages (varies)
  • Housing Australia (Home Guarantee Scheme): https://www.housingaustralia.gov.au/

First Home Owner Grant (FHOG)​

Definition
A state/territory grant for eligible first home buyers, often limited to new builds and subject to conditions.

Example
Eligibility and amounts differ by state.

References

  • State/Territory revenue office (FHOG rules vary)

First Home Guarantee (FHBG)​

Definition
A Home Guarantee Scheme product where an eligible first home buyer can purchase with a low deposit and avoid LMI, with the government providing a guarantee to the lender (subject to rules and caps).

Example
Eligible buyers may purchase with as little as a 5% deposit (scheme conditions apply).

References

  • Housing Australia (Home Guarantee Scheme): https://www.housingaustralia.gov.au/

Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee​

Definition
A guarantee pathway targeted to eligible regional first home buyers (names and settings can vary over time).

Example
May allow low-deposit purchase in eligible regional areas under scheme caps.

References

  • Housing Australia: https://www.housingaustralia.gov.au/

Family Home Guarantee​

Definition
A guarantee designed to support eligible single parents or guardians to buy a home with a low deposit and avoid LMI, subject to scheme settings.

Example
An eligible single parent may buy with a 2% deposit (scheme conditions apply).

References

  • Housing Australia: https://www.housingaustralia.gov.au/

Stamp Duty (Transfer Duty)​

Definition
A state/territory tax payable on property transfers, usually based on purchase price or dutiable value, with exemptions/concessions in some cases.

Example
Some first home buyers may receive concessions depending on state rules.

References

  • State/Territory revenue office (duty rules vary)

Concession (Stamp Duty)​

Definition
A reduction in stamp duty payable, often for eligible owner-occupiers or first home buyers.

Example
A partial concession may apply below a property value threshold.

References

  • State/Territory revenue office (varies)

Exemption (Stamp Duty)​

Definition
No stamp duty payable under certain eligibility conditions (state-based).

Example
Some new homes under a cap may qualify for exemption for eligible buyers.

References

  • State/Territory revenue office (varies)

Deposit​

Definition
The portion of the purchase price you pay upfront from your funds (or permitted sources), reducing the loan required.

Example
A 20% deposit typically avoids LMI for many lenders.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Genuine Savings​

Definition
Savings held and accumulated over time (as defined by lender policy), sometimes required to evidence saving behaviour at higher LVRs.

Example
Some lenders may require a portion of the deposit to be “genuine savings” over 3 months.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Gifted Deposit​

Definition
Funds given (often by family) toward the deposit, typically evidenced by a gift letter; some lenders accept with conditions.

Example
Parents gift $50,000 toward the deposit and sign a non-repayable gift declaration.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Guarantor​

Definition
Someone (often a parent) who provides a guarantee to support the borrower, typically using equity in their own property as additional security (product and risk vary by lender).

Example
A family guarantee may reduce or eliminate LMI by effectively lowering the lender’s exposure.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart (borrowing money / guarantees): https://moneysmart.gov.au/

Family Guarantee​

Definition
A guarantor arrangement where a family member provides security (usually limited) to assist with deposit/LVR.

Example
Guaranteeing part of the loan so the borrower can purchase with a smaller deposit.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/

Equity​

Definition
The difference between the property’s value and the outstanding loan balance.

Example
If your home is worth $900,000 and you owe $500,000, equity is $400,000.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Usable Equity​

Definition
Equity that may be accessible for borrowing, subject to lender policy and LVR limits.

Example
Borrowing up to 80% LVR against a property can free funds for renovations without LMI (depending on circumstances).

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Equity Release​

Definition
Increasing a loan against a property to access funds, typically for renovations, investments, or other purposes (subject to assessment).

Example
Refinancing from $400,000 to $500,000 to fund renovations.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Refinance​

Definition
Replacing an existing home loan with a new one (same or different lender), often to reduce rate, change features, or access equity.

Example
Switching lenders to get a lower variable rate and an offset account.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart (switching home loans): https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans/switching-home-loans

Cashback Offer​

Definition
A promotional payment some lenders offer for refinancing or new lending, usually with eligibility conditions.

Example
$2,000 cashback paid after settlement if conditions are met.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart (switching home loans): https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans/switching-home-loans

Loan Portability​

Definition
The ability to transfer an existing loan to a new property (subject to lender approval and valuation).

Example
Selling and buying a new home while keeping the same loan product.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Bridging Loan​

Definition
Short-term finance that can help cover the period when you buy a new property before selling your existing one (policy varies).

Example
Buying first, selling later; bridging finance covers overlap.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Settlement​

Definition
The legal completion of the purchase when funds are exchanged and title transfers to the buyer.

Example
Settlement is often 30–90 days after contract (varies).

References

  • State/Territory conveyancing guidance (varies)

Settlement Date​

Definition
The scheduled date the property purchase completes (can be negotiated in the contract).

Example
A 42-day settlement is common in some markets.

References

  • State/Territory conveyancing resources (varies)

Conveyancer​

Definition
A licensed professional who handles property transfer legal work (in some states a solicitor is used instead).

Example
Reviewing the contract, arranging title searches, coordinating settlement.

References

  • State/Territory consumer affairs / licensing bodies (varies)

Solicitor (Property)​

Definition
A lawyer who can manage conveyancing and provide legal advice on property contracts and settlement.

Example
A solicitor may advise on special conditions or contract risks.

References

  • State/Territory legal profession regulators (varies)

Contract of Sale​

Definition
The legal agreement to buy/sell the property, including price, conditions, and settlement date.

Example
May include finance clause, building & pest clause (where applicable).

References

  • State/Territory consumer property buying guidance (varies)

Finance Clause (Subject to Finance)​

Definition
A contract condition allowing the buyer to exit if they can’t obtain finance by a specified date (terms vary by contract).

Example
“Subject to finance approval within 14 days.”

References

  • State/Territory consumer property guidance (varies)

Cooling-Off Period​

Definition
A period after signing during which a buyer may cancel the contract (where applicable), often with a penalty; rules differ by state and by sale type.

Example
Cooling-off may not apply to auctions.

References

  • State/Territory fair trading / consumer affairs (varies)

Auction​

Definition
A sale method where the property is sold to the highest bidder above reserve. Contracts are typically unconditional at auction (state rules apply).

Example
You usually need loan pre-approval and deposit ready before auction.

References

  • State/Territory fair trading / consumer affairs (varies)

Reserve Price​

Definition
The minimum price the seller will accept at auction (often not disclosed).

Example
If bidding doesn’t reach reserve, property may be “passed in.”

References

  • State/Territory consumer affairs (auctions) (varies)

Passed In​

Definition
An auction outcome where bids don’t meet the reserve; the property isn’t sold at auction (negotiation may follow).

Example
Highest bidder may get first right to negotiate.

References

  • State/Territory consumer affairs (varies)

Deposit Bond​

Definition
A guarantee (often via an insurer/bank) used instead of paying the cash deposit at exchange, subject to vendor acceptance.

Example
Using a deposit bond for a 10% deposit when funds are tied up until settlement.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/

Building and Pest Inspection​

Definition
Independent inspection reports that can identify structural issues or pest damage (use and terminology vary by state and property type).

Example
Making the contract conditional on a satisfactory inspection.

References

  • State/Territory consumer property buying guidance (varies)

Strata (Owners Corporation / Body Corporate)​

Definition
A property ownership structure (common for apartments/townhouses) with shared ownership of common property and ongoing levies and rules.

Example
Quarterly strata levies for building maintenance and insurance.

References

  • State/Territory strata/owners corporation regulators (varies)

Strata Levies​

Definition
Ongoing fees paid by lot owners for maintenance, insurance, and administration of common property (amounts vary).

Example
Higher levies may apply for buildings with lifts, pools, or large sinking funds.

References

  • State/Territory strata regulators (varies)

Strata Report​

Definition
Documents about the strata scheme (minutes, finances, defects, by-laws), often reviewed during due diligence.

Example
Checking for special levies or major upcoming works.

References

  • State/Territory strata regulators (varies)

Special Levy​

Definition
An additional one-off strata levy charged to owners for major expenses not covered by the regular budget.

Example
$8,000 per lot to fund roof replacement.

References

  • State/Territory strata regulators (varies)

Sinking Fund (Capital Works Fund)​

Definition
A strata fund set aside for long-term maintenance and major works.

Example
Saving over time for repainting or lift replacement.

References

  • State/Territory strata regulators (varies)

Mortgage Registration Fee​

Definition
A government fee to register the mortgage on title (amount varies by state/territory).

Example
Paid at settlement as part of government charges.

References

  • State/Territory land titles office (varies)

Transfer Registration Fee​

Definition
A government fee to register the change of ownership on title (amount varies by state/territory).

Example
Paid at settlement.

References

  • State/Territory land titles office (varies)

Loan Purpose​

Definition
The reason for borrowing (purchase, refinance, construction, renovation, equity release), which can affect policy and pricing.

Example
Construction loans often use progress payments.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Construction Loan​

Definition
A loan used to build a property, typically paid in stages (progress payments) as construction milestones are completed.

Example
Funds released at slab, frame, lock-up, fit-out, completion (varies by lender/builder).

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Progress Payments (Drawdowns)​

Definition
Staged payments made by the lender during construction, usually after inspection and invoices.

Example
The lender pays the builder after the “frame” stage is verified.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Land Loan​

Definition
A loan used to buy vacant land; policies may differ from established property loans.

Example
Some lenders require building within a period to qualify for certain products.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

House and Land Package​

Definition
A purchase of land plus a building contract; lending often considers land settlement then construction draws.

Example
Two-part contract: land purchase first, build contract second.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Off-the-Plan (OTP)​

Definition
Buying a property before it’s built, based on plans; valuation and settlement timing risks can be higher.

Example
Bank valuation at completion may be lower than contract price, affecting LVR.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/

Sunset Clause​

Definition
A contract clause allowing termination if the property isn’t completed/settled by a certain date (rules vary by state and contract).

Example
If construction delays exceed the sunset date, parties may have rights to rescind.

References

  • State/Territory consumer property guidance (varies)

Loan Approval​

Definition
Formal acceptance by a lender to provide the loan under specified conditions (distinct from pre-approval).

Example
Formal approval issued after full verification and valuation.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Pre-Approval (Conditional Approval)​

Definition
An indication from a lender of how much you may be able to borrow, subject to conditions like verification, valuation, and property suitability.

Example
Pre-approval helps set a budget before making an offer but isn’t a guarantee.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Conditional Approval​

Definition
Approval subject to outstanding requirements (documents, valuation, employment confirmation, etc.).

Example
“Approved subject to valuation and payslips.”

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Unconditional Approval (Formal Approval)​

Definition
Approval where the lender has met conditions and is prepared to proceed to settlement (subject to standard settlement checks).

Example
Often required before removing a finance clause.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Loan Contract​

Definition
The legal agreement setting out loan terms, interest, fees, repayment obligations, and security arrangements.

Example
Includes details like rate type, offset eligibility, redraw rules, and default terms.

References

  • ASIC: https://asic.gov.au/

Mortgage Document​

Definition
The document creating the mortgage security interest over the property (registered on title).

Example
Signed as part of loan documentation and registered at settlement.

References

  • State/Territory land titles office (varies)

Direct Debit​

Definition
An automatic payment arrangement used to collect loan repayments from a nominated account.

Example
Repayments debited fortnightly on payday.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/

Repayment Frequency​

Definition
How often you pay (weekly, fortnightly, monthly). Paying more frequently can reduce interest slightly due to faster principal reduction (depending on product).

Example
Fortnightly repayments can be equivalent to 13 monthly repayments per year.

References

  • ASIC Moneysmart: https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans

Loan Repayment Holiday​

Definition
An arrangement to temporarily pause or reduce repayments, subject to lender approval and often with conditions and interest implications.

Example
Short-term hardship assistance may allow reduced repayments.

References

  • ASIC (financial hardship): https://asic.gov.au/

Hardship Assistance​

Definition
Support offered by lenders if you’re struggling to meet repayments, which may include repayment changes; governed by consumer credit laws.

Example
Temporarily switching to interest-only or extending the term.

References

  • ASIC (financial hardship): https://asic.gov.au/

Default​

Definition
Failing to meet loan obligations (e.g., missing repayments), which can lead to fees, enforcement action, or credit reporting impacts.

Example
Multiple missed payments may trigger default notices.

References

  • ASIC: https://asic.gov.au/

Arrears​

Definition
