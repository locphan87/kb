Glossary

TermDescription
AI, AGI, AND ACIArtificial intelligence (AI) is the science of teaching machines to learn humanlike capabilities. Artificial general intelligence (AGI) is the point at which an AI can perform all human cognitive skills better than the smartest humans. ACI, or artificial capable intelligence, is a fast-approaching point between AI and AGI: ACI can achieve a wide range of complex tasks but is still a long way from being fully general.
CONTAINMENTThe ability to monitor, curtail, control, and potentially even close down technologies.
FOUR FEATURESThe unique characteristics of the coming wave that exacerbate the challenge of containment. They are asymmetry, hyper-evolution, omni-use, and autonomy.
FRAGILITY AMPLIFIERSApplications and impacts of coming-wave technologies that will shake the already brittle foundations of the nation-state.
PESSIMISM AVERSIONThe tendency for people, particularly elites, to ignore, downplay, or reject narratives they see as overly negative. A variant of optimism bias, it colors much of the debate around the future, especially in technology circles.
SYNTHETIC BIOLOGYThe ability to design and engineer new organisms or redesign existing biological systems.
TECHNOLOGYThe application of scientific knowledge (in the broadest possible sense) to produce tools or practical outcomes.
THE COMING WAVEAn emerging cluster of related technologies centered on AI and synthetic biology whose transformative applications will both empower humankind and present unprecedented risks.
THE CONTAINMENT PROBLEMTechnology’s predisposition to diffuse widely in waves and to have emergent impacts that are impossible to predict or control, including negative and unforeseen consequences.
THE DILEMMAThe growing likelihood that both new technologies and even their absence might lead to catastrophic and/or dystopian outcomes.
THE GRAND BARGAINIn exchange for a monopoly over the right to use force, citizens expect nation-states to preserve order and provide public services, including by harnessing new technologies while minimizing the harmful side effects.
THE NARROW PATHThe potential for humankind to strike a balance between openness and closure when it comes to containing the technologies in the coming wave that avoids catastrophic or dystopian outcomes.
WAVESThe global diffusion or proliferation of a generation of technology anchored in a new general-purpose technology.
ChatGPTis a language model developed by OpenAI. It is a variant of the GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) model, which is trained on a massive amount of internet text data to generate human-like text. The model is fine-tuned for specific tasks, such as language translation, text summarization, and conversation. With its ability to understand and respond to natural language inputs, ChatGPT can be used for a variety of applications, such as chatbots, virtual assistants, and language-based games.
Github CopilotCopilot is an AI-powered coding assistant developed by GitHub. It is a system that aims to help developers write code more efficiently and effectively by suggesting code completions. Copilot uses machine learning to understand the code you're working on, and suggests completions based on the context of the code, taking into account the structure of the repository and the contents of the file you're working on. Copilot suggests completions for variables, functions, and more, based on the context of the code you're working on.
  • "fuifv": An acronym, often jokingly used by Microsoft engineers in the 1990s, standing for "I'm fully vested," indicating they had received all their stock options and might be less motivated.
  • Acqui-hiring: The practice of acquiring a company primarily to obtain its talented employees, often shutting down the original product.
  • Android: Google's free, open-source operating system predominantly used for mobile phones and tablets.
  • Bell Labs: A historic research and development institution, known for significant innovations like the transistor, lasers, and solar cells.
  • Caffeine (Google Search): A major improvement to Google Search in 2010 that made results 50 percent "fresher" by increasing indexing speed.
  • Casual Collisions: Intentional design of physical spaces, like cafeterias and microkitchens, to encourage unplanned interactions and conversations between employees from different teams.
  • Centithread/Millithread (Kilothread): Terms used at Google for extremely long email conversations on sensitive topics.
  • Chromebook: Laptops powered by Google's Chrome operating system.
  • Code Health: The practice of maintaining the overall sustainability and scalability of software code to minimize complexity and ensure long-term functionality.
  • Conscientiousness: A personality trait characterized by being diligent, careful, and thorough; a key attribute Google seeks in hires.
  • Culture Eats Strategy for Breakfast: A saying emphasizing that a strong, positive organizational culture is more impactful and foundational than any strategic plan.
  • Deliberate Practice: A highly structured and focused method of practice aimed at improving specific skills, often uncomfortable and requiring intense effort.
  • Deru Kugi Wa Utareru: A Japanese proverb meaning "The stake that sticks up gets hammered down," used in the text to signify a warning to conform.
  • Founder Mentality: The concept that individuals, regardless of their formal role, should act with the ownership, vision, and responsibility typically associated with a company founder.
  • Gaussian Distribution (Normal Distribution): A common probability distribution that shows data symmetrically around a mean, often referred to as a bell curve. Contrasted with power law distribution in the text.
  • Gnothi Seauton (Know Thyself): A Delphic maxim emphasizing self-knowledge, used in the text to highlight the challenge of objective self-awareness in decision-making.
  • Google Doodles: The special, temporary alterations of the Google logo on its homepage, often celebrating holidays, events, or notable figures.
  • Google Play Store: Google's digital distribution service for Android applications, games, music, movies, and books.
  • Google[x]: Google's semi-secret research and development facility, responsible for projects like Glass and self-driving cars.
  • Googlegeist: Google's comprehensive annual employee survey used to gather feedback on various aspects of the company culture, management, and work environment.
  • Googleyness: A set of cultural attributes that Google seeks in its job candidates, including enjoyment of fun, intellectual humility, conscientiousness, comfort with ambiguity, and evidence of courageous life paths.
  • Intellectual Humility: The willingness to admit one might be wrong or doesn't know everything, considered a crucial trait for learning and growth at Google.
  • Latency: In the context of search, refers to how quickly search results appear.
  • Local Maximum: In mathematics, the highest value within a specific, constrained range of values, distinct from the absolute highest value (global maximum).
  • Mass Customization: A production approach where goods and services are designed to meet individual customer needs with the efficiency of mass production.
  • Microkitchens: Small, informal kitchen areas interspersed throughout Google offices, providing snacks, drinks, and a space for employees to relax and connect.
  • Nudges: Subtle interventions or changes in the environment that influence people's choices and behaviors without restricting options or significantly altering economic incentives.
  • OKRs (Objectives and Key Results): A goal-setting methodology where objectives are ambitious goals and key results are specific, measurable, and verifiable metrics.
  • Open-Source: Denotes software whose source code is freely available to the public for use, modification, and distribution.
  • Peer Bonus: A system at Google where employees can give small monetary bonuses to colleagues as a thank you for above-and-beyond contributions.
  • Power Law Distribution: A statistical distribution where a small number of events account for a large proportion of the total outcomes, often seen in phenomena like wealth distribution or actor earnings.
  • Sample Bias: A statistical error that occurs when a sample used for analysis is not representative of the population, leading to inaccurate conclusions.
  • Sarariman: A Japanese term for a salaried worker, often associated with a traditional, stable, and rule-following career path.
  • Scalability: The ability of a solution to work equally well for a small number of users and a very large number of users.
  • Speak2Tweet: A product created by Googlers and Twitter engineers to allow Egyptians to communicate during the 2011 internet shutdown by transcribing voice messages into Tweets.
  • Structural Holes: A concept in sociology referring to the gaps or lack of connections between different social groups, where innovation is often found to occur.
  • Summly: A news summarization app founded by Nick D'Aloisio, acquired by Yahoo in an example of "acqui-hiring."
  • Thin Slices: Refers to the phenomenon where people make quick, initial assessments or judgments based on very limited information or short observations.
  • Wegmans: A supermarket chain mentioned as an example of a successful company with a strong employee-centric approach similar to Google's.
  • Work Sample Test: An assessment method used in hiring where candidates are asked to perform tasks that are representative of the actual job.
  • YouTube: A Google-owned video-sharing platform.
